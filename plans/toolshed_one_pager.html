§1

What It Is

A neighborhood tool-lending ledger where the record cannot be ambiguous, altered, or hosted away. Each tool is a Replica; who holds it is an authoritative per-field Cap; a borrow is a Cap transfer co-signed by both parties at the physical handoff. Custody is computed from op presence in the DAG — never stored, never asserted, never a matter of memory or he-said-she-said.

The ledger does not prevent theft. It makes accountability unambiguous — every custody change is a dual-signed, causally ordered, auditable op that both parties consented to at the moment of handoff. That is the entire claim, and the substrate already proves it. The honesty constraint that defines this cut

Toolshed is a sibling of Treehouse (PD-002), not a replacement: same M1+M2 dependency line, same fence discipline, different wedge. Treehouse sells durable communication; Toolshed sells unambiguous custody. They share a substrate, an audience overlap (the platform-burned), and an endgame — the neighbors who share a ladder are the residents who later deliberate a local matter. Toolshed → Township is the adoption story.

§2

The Core Move: Proximity, Not Location

The naive design reaches for GPS to "prove" where a tool is. That primitive is rejected here, twice over: GPS is spoofable at the API layer and at the radio layer (commodity SDR), and the only fix — hardware attestation via Play Integrity / App Attest — roots trust in Google and Apple certificate authorities. Building A3 resistance on the two largest platform landlords is not an adversary tax; it is the substrate contradicting itself. It also excludes de-Googled devices — precisely the hardware the target audience carries.

The reframe: custody transfer never needed to know where the tool is. It needs to know both parties were present at the handoff. Presence is a co-signature problem, and co-signatures are pure M1 machinery.

The dual-signed handoff op — schematic
op :custody_transfer
  replica:   tool:ladder-6ft
  field:     :holder            // authoritative per-field Cap
  from:      did:key:lender      // current validated holder
  to:        did:key:borrower
  deps:      [ ...causal frontier ]
  sig_from:  ed25519(lender)     // exchanged in person:
  sig_to:    ed25519(borrower)   // QR scan or NFC tap at the door
Neither party can unilaterally claim a transfer happened or didn't. The return is the same op in reverse — and by the Q-06 rule, a return request is a durable op resolved by the current validated holder, so a lender who won't acknowledge a genuine return leaves a visible, timestamped refusal in the record. The audit trail is the dispute resolution.
The R-04 field note — physical handoff as Sybil cost

Sybil-resistant admission is the program's registered open question (R-04). Toolshed contributes an empirical data point: at neighborhood scale, admission cost is denominated in physical presence — a fleet of fake identities cannot take possession of a ladder, because a human must hand it over at a door. Vouch-gated admission here collapses to "a neighbor physically vouched," and the scammer's two real attacks — fake identity at admission, ambiguity at handoff — are dissolved by the vouch and the co-signature respectively, before any exotic machinery is reached. This observation feeds the R-04 register regardless of whether the product ships.

§3

What Ships

Ships · exercises M1 (≈ Township W0)
Join by Vouch

Admission is a Cap granted by an existing neighbor — invite as an attenuated, TTL'd capability, generated on-device, typically handed over in person. No signup, no registry, no address harvest.

Ships · exercises M1 + M2 (≈ Township W1)
The Shed Inventory

Each listed tool is a Replica: convergent description, condition notes, and availability, offline-editable, partition-and-heal without a coordinator. The neighborhood's inventory survives every device dying but one.

Ships · exercises M1 (≈ Township W2, sharpened)
Dual-Signed Custody

Borrow and return as co-signed :custody_transfer ops via QR/NFC at the handoff. Due-back as a caveat on the borrow Cap (TTL, the V1 macaroon model doing product work). Overdue is computed, not nagged into existence.

Ships · exercises M1 (≈ Township W2)
Stewardship & Succession

A shed steward role as a transferable Cap. The neighbor who started the shed moves away; the steward Cap transfers in one op; their later stale op quarantines visibly. The shed outlives its founder — the exact succession machinery Township needs, soaked at garden-fence stakes.

The demo storyline — Toolshed edition

  1. A block starts a shed; neighbors join by vouch Cap handed over literally across a fence. Identities generate on-device; nothing is hosted.
  2. A resident lists a tile saw with photos and a condition note, from a phone, offline; the listing converges when devices next meet.
  3. A borrow: two phones tap at the door, both parties sign one op, a due-back caveat rides the Cap. The custody record is unambiguous from the instant of handoff.
  4. A dispute: "I returned it." The DAG answers — either the dual-signed return op exists or it doesn't, and if a return request sits unresolved, the refusal is visible and timestamped. No ambiguity survives contact with the audit trail.
  5. The founder moves to another state. Stewardship transfers by Cap; the inventory, custody history, and every open loan continue from the remaining devices. Nothing was hosted, so nothing was lost.
§4

The Honest Adversary Model

Adversary · A1
The Individual — N/A

No one coerces a neighbor over a hedge trimmer. Custody records are deliberately legible — unambiguity is the product. Receipt-freeness would be actively wrong here.

POSTURE → out of scope, and inverted: Toolshed is the legibility end of the A1 spectrum, the honest opposite corner from Cadence's unprovable vouches. The Lattice.Attestation seam stays stubbed.
Adversary · A2-lite
The Scammer

The real neighborhood threat: fake identities joining to walk off with tools, and handoff ambiguity exploited in disputes. Not entryism — opportunism.

DEFENSE → vouch-gated admission priced in physical presence · dual-signed handoffs · deps-decidable quarantine of stale-holder ops · legible authority chains. Honest limit: a vouched-in neighbor can still steal a ladder; the ledger makes them unambiguously accountable, not honest.
Adversary · A3-lite
The Platform

The incumbent is a Facebook group and a text thread — records that vanish with an account ban, a group deletion, or a policy change. The Buy Nothing app migration burned this exact population once already.

DEFENSE → the full A3 defense, inherited for free: local-first, coordinator-free, nothing hosted. Strengthened here by the explicit refusal of platform attestation roots — the trust anchor is the neighbors' own keys, not Mountain View's CA.
Adversary tax — what this cut costs the program

A1: untouched; if anything, clarifying — Toolshed plants a flag at the legibility corner of the surface, sharpening the ADR-RW-001 "same surface, opposite corner" framing with an in-house example. A2: strengthened — the physical-presence admission cost is a live R-04 experiment. A3: strengthened — refusing GPS/attestation keeps the trust-anchor claim clean and forces M2's carrier to be honest about what a phone realm can verify without a platform's blessing. The real tax, as with PD-002, is attention: QR/NFC handoff UX, photos and listings, reminder ergonomics — product labor that advances no milestone gate. Priced, not hidden.

Exit gate — when Toolshed counts as real

① Two unaffiliated neighborhoods (≥8 households each) run real lending on it for 90 days with zero server dependency in the core loop. ② ≥50 dual-signed custody transfers occur in the wild, including at least one full borrow→overdue→return cycle resolved by the record alone. ③ One real dispute is settled by audit trail without either party being able to contest what the DAG shows. ④ One steward succession fires in the wild and the inventory plus all open loans continue cleanly. ⑤ The handoff interaction is measured under ten seconds phone-to-phone — the friction constraint is a gate, not a wish.

Honesty caveats — read before believing the one-pager

The competition is a text message and existing trust: if logging a borrow costs more effort than remembering it, the ledger dies of friction — which is why gate ⑤ is a hard gate and the tap must be the entire UX. The ledger makes accountability unambiguous; it does not make neighbors honest, and copy must never imply theft prevention. The dual-signature op shape is a small but real extension to the M1 op model and needs its ADR before any code. NFC/QR co-signing UX across iOS and Android has platform quirks (iOS NFC entitlements in particular) that belong to product labor, not the substrate — but they are real labor. The low op cadence that keeps D-03's compaction cliff distant also means organic soak is slow: 90 days of a quiet shed exercises fewer edge cases than a week of a chatty Treehouse, so the two siblings stress different things and neither substitutes for the other. And the working name is a placeholder; the fence is load-bearing, not the label.
