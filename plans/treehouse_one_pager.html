Treehouse

The low-stakes product hiding inside the Township stack: durable, member-held community spaces for people already burned by platforms — shipped on M1+M2 alone, with every research-gated milestone fenced out. Working name; the seam matters more than the sign.

DEPENDS · M1 ✓ + M2 EXCLUDES · M4 / M6 ENTIRELY CADENCE-ADJACENT · NOT CADENCE JULY 2026 · ONE-PAGER
§1

What It Is

A Discord-shaped space whose record its members hold themselves. Threads are Replicas; roles are Caps; the community's history lives on its members' devices and converges when they do. A relay carries the ops and keeps a plaintext copy: its operator, or for a member-operated relay the host device and its OS (including any administrator), its backups and every transport peer the manifest admits, can read that copy, and the host can withhold availability. What the relay cannot do is decide semantic authority or erase the history held on members' devices. Everything user-facing runs on behaviors that are already green (M1) plus the M2 carrier and persistence.

Treehouse sells exactly what the substrate already proves: durability precedes liveness, and authority is a revocable capability, not a role in someone's database. It sells nothing that is gated on open research. The honesty constraint that defines this cut

It is not Township. Township is civic, coercion-facing, and gated on M4. Treehouse is social, platform-facing, and gated on nothing but engineering and product labor. The two share a substrate and a scope discipline; they do not share a threat model.

§2

Who It's For

Not "communities" in general — free incumbents win that fight on polish. The wedge is people who have already paid the platform tax and self-selected into caring. They tolerate rough UX, they evangelize, and they actually exercise the behaviors (succession, revocation, partition-heal) that need real-world soak.

Audience · 01
Open-Source Projects

Maintainer teams and contributor communities coordinating in Discord servers and free Slack workspaces they don't control. Governance disputes, maintainer burnout, and ownership handoffs are routine events here — succession is a feature they already need, not a hypothetical.

PAIN: history behind Slack's retention wall · server owned by a departed founder · mod account compromise
Audience · 02
The Fediverse Diaspora

Mastodon and Bluesky users chose decentralization once already and then watched instances shut down, defederate, or change hands. They are ideologically pre-sold on records their members hold themselves, and fluent in the tradeoffs.

PAIN: instance shutdowns orphaning communities · admin unilateralism · migration losing history and graph
Audience · 03
The Platform-Burned

Communities that survived a Discord ban wave, a subreddit closure, or an ownership coup. Small population, maximal motivation — and the only users who will honestly stress-test "the record a phone already synced survives every other device dying."

PAIN: one moderation decision by a platform erased years of community record and structure
§3

What Ships

The Township workloads, minus the one that needs research. Same seams, same modules, lower stakes.

Ships · exercises M1 + M2 (≈ Township W1)
Durable Threads

Channels and threads as Replicas: convergent posts and edits, offline authorship, partition-and-heal whose conflict reduction is deterministic and so needs no coordinator and no conflict dialog. Transport still runs through a relay. History is device-held and replayable rather than sitting behind a 90-day retention wall, and the relay keeps a plaintext copy: its operator, or for a member-operated relay the host device and its OS including any administrator, that device's backups and every transport peer the relay manifest admits, can read that copy; the host can withhold availability; and it cannot erase the copies on members' devices. The first beta stops new posting at 4,000 ops, 8 MiB or any cold open over five seconds and rolls the thread over into a new one under the same Space, so this is not a safe-unbounded-history claim.

Ships · exercises M0 + M1 (≈ Township W2)
Roles as Caps

Moderator and admin authority as transferable per-field Caps. Handing off a community is one op. Founder loss is not survived today: AF-2 fails because beacons are honored only from the replica root, witnessed recovery covers a role and not the root, and key rotation and recovery are M3; manual admin transfer is the only handoff the first beta claims. A founder who loses their device is not recovered by this cut; that is the AF-2 gate, and it fails today.

Ships · exercises M1 (≈ Township W2)
Bounded Blast Radius

Zero implicit authority as the anti-takeover story: a phished mod account holds only its explicit, attenuated, revocable Caps — no silent webhook powers, no ambient admin. One revoke severs the credential and the live path; the quarantine of stale-holder ops is visible in the trust graph.

Ships · exercises M1 (≈ Township W0)
Invite by Capability

Admission is a Cap granted by existing members (the V1 macaroon model doing product work). An invitation is recipient-bound and has one signed ID and one recipient; replay is idempotent, rebinding is quarantined, and revocation closes it; it is not a bearer link and carries no expiry or use-limit claim. No account creation ceremony. The relay holds the admitted transport peer list and a plaintext log rather than accounts: its operator, or for a member-operated relay the host device and its OS including any administrator, that device's backups and every transport peer the relay manifest admits, can read that log, and the host can withhold availability.

The demo storyline — Treehouse edition

  1. An OSS project spins up a space; contributors join by recipient-bound invitation generated on-device. No signup, no email harvest. A member-operated or operator-hosted relay carries the ops and keeps a plaintext copy that its host can read and withhold; it cannot decide who holds a role or erase what members' devices hold.
  2. A design thread accumulates posts from laptops that are mostly offline; two maintainers edit the RFC summary during a partition; the heal converges silently.
  3. The founding maintainer steps away. The admin Cap transfers in one op; their later stale moderation op is quarantined and audited, visibly.
  4. A mod account is phished. The attacker holds exactly one scoped Cap; revocation is one op; the trust graph shows precisely what the compromised account touched and when. The audit is the incident report.
  5. Slack's free tier would have eaten this history eight months ago. History is device-held and replayable: wipe every device but one and the record and role structure that phone had synced survive on it, with the relay's plaintext copy, readable by its operator or member-operated host device and OS administrators, that device's backups and every transport peer the manifest admits, as a second copy that lasts only while that host keeps serving it, since the host can withhold availability. A phone that never pulled an acknowledged op loses that op (the AF-1 stale-copy control).
§4

The Honest Adversary Model

Per house rule, this cut states which adversaries it faces and which defense it taxes. The pattern is deliberate: Treehouse faces weakened versions of A2 and A3, and faces A1 not at all.

Adversary · A1
The Individual — N/A

No one coerces a knitting circle or a maintainer team over vouches. Coercion resistance buys nothing here and its cost (legibility, M4 machinery) buys negative value.

POSTURE → out of scope. The Lattice.Attestation seam stays stubbed. Treehouse makes no receipt-freeness claim, ever, in any copy.
Adversary · A2-lite
The Insider & The Phisher

Not entryism at civic stakes — mod drama, ownership coups, compromised accounts, scam-wave takeovers. Discord's actual daily failure mode.

DEFENSE → zero implicit authority, one-op revocation, deps-decidable quarantine (V-01), legible authority chains. Honest limit: Lattice bounds what a compromised identity can do; it does not detect the phish or filter the scam link.
Adversary · A3-lite
The Platform

Not the state with a subpoena — the landlord with a retention policy. Slack's 90-day wall, Discord's ban lever, an instance admin's shutdown notice: A3 events that happen to ordinary communities on schedule.

DEFENSE → local-first, deterministic conflict reduction that needs no coordinator, and honest about the host. The relay is a plaintext host: its operator, or for a member-operated relay the host device and its OS including any administrator, that device's backups and every transport peer the relay manifest admits, can read the log; the host can withhold availability; and the relay cannot decide semantic authority or erase device-held history. Losing the relay is the AF-1 gate, which holds today; the record leaves with the members' devices.
Adversary tax — what this cut costs the program

A1: untouched — the stub seam means nothing here forecloses M4. A2: mildly strengthened — real-world soak of quarantine, revocation, and succession hardens the exact mechanisms Township's civic roles depend on. A3: strengthened — M2's carrier and persistence get production pressure early. The real tax is attention: product labor (UX, notifications, mobile, media) that does not advance any milestone gate, and a public artifact whose rough edges will be read as the substrate's rough edges. Priced, not hidden.

Exit gate — when Treehouse counts as real

① Three unaffiliated communities (≥1 OSS project) run their primary coordination on it for 60 consecutive days; the relay is a member-operated or operator-hosted plaintext host whose host can read the log and withhold availability, and that the core loop tolerates losing (AF-1), not a dependency the copy pretends away. The first beta runs one community for two weeks (Plan 158); this larger gate is deferred. ② At least one real admin transfer and one real revocation event occur in the wild and audit cleanly in the trust graph; the witnessed succession ceremony is hidden in the first beta. ③ A full-partition week (member devices offline, no rendezvous) heals to convergence with no data loss and no conflict dialog. ④ A skeptical outsider can verify the durability claim from the exported audit evidence alone.

Honesty caveats — read before believing the one-pager

The gap between this substrate and a usable product is UX labor, not cryptography — notifications, mobile ergonomics, media handling, and onboarding are where the effort actually goes, and none of it advances a milestone gate. Competing with free, polished incumbents on convenience is a losing frame; the only viable pitch is the one the incumbents structurally cannot make. "Bounded blast radius" is an authority claim, not an anti-phishing claim — Treehouse does not detect scams, and copy must never imply it does. Unbounded log growth (D-03) arrives faster in a chatty social space than in civic deliberation; the compaction cliff should be assumed closer, not farther. And the working name is a placeholder — what is load-bearing is the fence, not the label.
