04 / Recorded policies and proposed amendments

Keep the promises precise.

Plaintext is disclosed

The relay operator, host administrators, backups and admitted peers can read their copies. The host can deny service. Member device hosting is an option, not an entry gate. Every live new Thread and rollover successor still needs an assisted provisioning operator.

Facts travel by consent

No score or negative broadcast. A subject presents selected receipts and signs a fresh challenge with the receipt key. Cross-shed identity stays deferred. Losing the signing key prevents new same-key presentations of earlier receipts; the old signed evidence remains verifiable.

Rollover preserves history

Warn before 4,000 ops / 8 MiB / five seconds. Archive and create a successor. Twelve total Thread slots include archives: roughly 48,000 total operations, including overhead, or about 320 per person at 150 members. Exhaustion closes posting; R35 gates expanded support.

Renewal cannot die with the founder

Witnessed time can expire leases. It cannot renew an expired founder-only delegation chain or constrain every successor grant by itself. R02/R04 settle and prove the continuing authority path.

A new relay cannot authorize itself

Catalog recovery currently needs a root ceremony. R11 requires narrow authority established at group creation, retained trust anchors and forged-replacement negatives.

Concurrent offline posts can overshoot

A local warning is not a global quota. R15 defines late posts, bounded inbound evidence and explicit copying to the successor, without rewriting a signed op.

A new key cannot sign as an old key

A social link preserves attribution. It does not prove possession of a lost receipt key. R29 shows that distinction and the limits of selected evidence.

R01 requires explicit adoption: archive vocabulary and membership rules, one-app isolation, declared dependency reversals, an Android witness-ceremony amendment and scoped §4a un-parking. Historical pinned status strings stay intact. LAN stays parked; iOS requires R32a approval. R19a and R25 separately amend their later semantics. No signature or approval is fabricated.

06 / How work closes

Small handoffs. Honest finish lines.

For every packet

  1. Read the current source plans and recheck the base and merged blockers.
  2. Use an isolated worktree and explicit ownership of shared files.
  3. Prove the public behavior and refusal cases; keep both runtimes aligned.
  4. Review the exact diff, resolve findings and verify PR and merge-result CI.
  5. Record evidence, limits and the next ready packets.

Do not hide external gates

  1. Contract adoption precedes changed identity and authority behavior.
  2. Live operation needs host, DNS, backups, credentials and an operator.
  3. Physical proof needs signed artifacts and independent phones/witnesses.
  4. Pilots need participants, disclosure, support and observation time.
  5. iOS credentials and device hosting remain separate optional lanes.
