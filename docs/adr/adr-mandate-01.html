1 · CONTEXT

What forced the decision

Standard capability delegation flows downward and attenuating: a holder grants a narrower slice of what it already has, and keeps the whole. That models permission. It does not model constitution — the relationship where an aggregate's authority is not lent to it as an errand but brought into being by the standing consent of its members, and ceases when that consent is withdrawn.

A reporter mandates a newsroom. The newsroom mandates a news DAO. At every level, authority is not a stored field the aggregate possesses — it is a quantity the aggregate derives, per question, from the live mandates beneath it. Withdraw the mandate and the derived authority recomputes downward. That is the state deriving its power from the people, made into an op.

This needs a primitive distinct from Cap. Reusing the Cap name would corrupt both: a Cap attenuates and is retained by its issuer; a Mandate does neither. It is a new object with two hard properties — it flows up the aggregation hierarchy, and it is constitutive rather than task-scoped.

2 · DECISION

One grantor, one weight. Fixed scope.

Two forks decided together, because the second only stays clean given the first.

Decision A — the weight model
Set-membership over transferable weight

A mandate is a membership fact, not a conserved substance. Authority-at-a-level is |live mandates satisfying the scope predicate| — a fold over a set, idempotent and commutative, which the DAG converges on with no new invariant. A grantor either is-mandating a grantee for a scope, or is not. There is no fractional standing, no "60% of my voice," nothing to split, transfer, or double-spend.

Rejected: transferable weight (a scalar a grantor holds and can apportion). It reintroduces a conserved quantity, drags in the delegative-democracy / Liquid Democracy attack surface (delegation cycles, super-voter concentration, weight-buying via transfer), and slides toward token mechanics — an explicit non-goal of Township.

Decision B — the scope model
Fixed atom over predicate

A mandate binds to exactly one scope, drawn from a station vocabulary (e.g. :editorial_standards_vote). Consent is granted per scope. A new scope an aggregate invents later starts with zero mandates until members opt in — which is not a limitation but the correct behaviour: consent must not auto-extend to questions no one agreed to.

Rejected (for the pilot): a mandate matching a predicate over questions. More expressive, but it immediately reintroduces the "did I consent to this interpretation of my grant" ambiguity that written constitutions spend centuries litigating. Ship the narrow thing; make scope-generality a documented non-goal.

3 · SHAPE

Mandate is not a Cap

Existing · L1
Cap
  • Flows down a delegation chain
  • Attenuates — each hop narrows
  • Issuer retains the full authority
  • Grants a task: "you may do X"
  • Revocation severs a granted slice
New · L2
Mandate
  • Flows up the aggregation hierarchy
  • Does not attenuate — it is presence or absence
  • Grantor contributes to what the grantee's authority is
  • Grants constitution: "you may derive authority from my standing consent"
  • Revocation is unilateral — needs no counter-sign, cannot be vetoed

Op-shape

Two ops on the existing 2.0 authority DAG. Nothing new in the substrate.

# mandate.grant — signed by the grantor's key alone
{
  :kind        => :authority,        # existing op kind
  :sub         => :mandate_grant,
  :grantor     => grantor_pubkey,     # did:key-class; NOT a user row
  :grantee     => grantee_replica_id, # the newsroom's Replica
  :scope       => :editorial_standards_vote,
  :expires_at  => nil                  # nil = standing consent
}

# mandate.revoke — UNILATERAL. only the grantor's key authorizes it.
{
  :kind        => :authority,
  :sub         => :mandate_revoke,
  :grantor     => grantor_pubkey,     # must match the grant's grantor
  :grant_ref   => granted_op_hash     # the mandate being withdrawn
}

Authority is a read, never a stored field — the line that makes this democracy rather than a table of weighted signatures:

# A realm's authority over a scope is a pure fold over live mandates.
# Deps-decidable, identical across replicas => property (d) holds unchanged.
standing(grantee, scope) =
  count(mandates where grantee == grantee
                   and scope   == scope
                   and state   == :live)
4 · WHY IT COSTS ALMOST NOTHING

The machinery already exists

Mandate needsProvided by
Signed grant / revokeThe :authority op kind and canonical signing (Q-01/Q-02) — a mandate is just an authority op with a new :sub.
Unilateral revoke, no vetoOne policy: the revoke op validates iff signed by the same key as the grant. The grantee has no approve_revocation action because there is nothing to approve.
Withdrawal defeats dependent actsThe Q-03 quarantine cascade, pointed at mandates: any grantee action whose validity depended on a now-revoked mandate is quarantined the instant the revoke is causally observed — exactly V-01's "did the author causally observe the authority-changing op."
Convergent count across replicasProperty (a) convergence + property (d) identical quarantine sets. Set-membership is order-independent, so the fold is byte-identical under property (c).

The op-shape is specifiable against M1's machinery today. It is not an M1 primitive — but it takes no new substrate, only a new :sub and one policy.

5 · CONSEQUENCE

The attack classes this deletes

The value of Decision A is mostly in what becomes unexpressible. You defend these by subtraction, not by mechanism.

  1. Weight-buying via transfer

    You cannot sell "40% of my standing" because standing is not a scalar you hold. No conserved substance ⇒ no market in it.

  2. Delegation cycles & super-voters

    The Liquid Democracy pathologies (A mandates B mandates A; one node accreting transferred weight) have no surface — membership does not chain into concentration.

  3. Silent re-mandating

    A grantee cannot forward a grantor's mandate upward without the grantor's fresh consent, because the grantor's key is the only thing that can emit a grant. Consent is transitive only through explicit re-granting at each level.

  4. Scope creep by reinterpretation

    Fixed-atom scope (Decision B) means a mandate for one question can never be counted toward another. New questions start empty.

6 · THE TAX

What this strengthens, and what it charges

Per the PD-001 rule: every mechanism that strengthens one adversary defense states which of the other two it taxes. The Mandate is strongly A3-resistant — there is no authority to seize, and consent-withdrawal dissolves acts that rode on it. It taxes the other two.

A2 — the faction · this is where the whole threat concentrates

If authority is count(live mandates), then manufacturing grantors is the takeover. Set-membership does not defend Sybil — it makes admission the entire game. A grantee that fabricates 10,000 grantor keys and self-mandates has "constituted" enormous authority, perfectly validly from the DAG's view. The primitive presupposes a real, Sybil-resistant grantor set; it does not produce one. Whoever answers "who is a real reporter" holds the actual power.

A1 — the coercer · aggregation concentrates the target

Compelling one grantor's key compels one voice. Compelling a grantee's fold compels everyone who mandated it, at once, invisibly. Upward aggregation is a coercion-concentration surface. Township's answer is scale + social recourse (the equivocation branch, M4); the recursion to DAO scale is not covered by this ADR.

Where the hard part actually lives

This is not a flaw in the Mandate — it is the Mandate being honest about where the difficulty is. One-grantor-one-weight does not solve Sybil; it relocates the entire problem onto admission and makes that relocation visible. That is a feature: the ADR names exactly one unsolved thing instead of smuggling it into a weighting scheme.

At Township scale the answer already exists — vouch-gated admission, one shared verifier, a human community that would notice 10,000 phantom reporters. That is precisely why the reporter → newsroom level is prototypable now (M5) and the newsroom → DAO recursion is deferred: it is the federation seam (M6) wearing new clothes. "Does newsroom-local mandate → DAO-federated mandate compose or leak?" is R-06.

7 · SCOPE FENCE

Deliberately excluded

Not in this ADR — do not implement under its authority
  • Transferable / fractional weight, and everything downstream of it (Liquid Democracy, quadratic mechanisms, token accounting).
  • Predicate-scoped mandates. Fixed atom only.
  • Sybil-resistant admission — the grantor set is assumed real; constituting it is Township's vouch layer (separate), not this primitive.
  • The newsroom → DAO recursion as a shipped mechanism. Its op-shape composes trivially; whether it composes without leaking is M6 / R-06.
  • Coercion-resistance of upward aggregation (M4 equivocation branch).
  • Nested-revocation cardinality leak ("reporter X withdrew from newsroom Y" is observable) — flagged, measured at Township scale, not solved here (R-05).

If any excluded item starts looking necessary inside the pilot, the requirement is questioned before the machinery is added — the standing "do-not-implement" discipline.

Decision: accept the narrow Mandate — set-membership, fixed-scope, unilateral revoke — as an L2 application-seam primitive prototyped at Township (reporter → newsroom, single level, one shared verifier). Recursion and Sybil-resistant admission are named, deferred, and mapped to existing gates (M4/M6, R-04/R-05/R-06). No new substrate; one new authority-op :sub and one revoke policy.

References: PD-001 §1 (three-adversary Pareto surface), §6 Q-03 (quarantine cascade), V-01 (one predicate for stale-holder + revocation), R-04/R-05/R-06 (Sybil, cardinality, federation seam).
